Epidemiology & Prognosis

Disease Overview

EAC is the fastest-rising GI cancer in Western countries, with incidence increasing ~7.8%/year from 1973–1999. Most patients present at advanced stage.

Risk factors
Barrett's esophagus (primary precursor), chronic GERD, obesity, smoking. Barrett's carries 0.3–0.5%/year risk of malignant transformation. No early-stage symptoms — the disease is usually silent until T2+.
Why late presentation dominates
Dysphagia — the cardinal symptom — does not appear until the esophageal lumen is 50–70% obstructed, typically T3 or beyond. Barrett's surveillance programs detect a minority; most patients have no prior diagnosis.
The recurrence problem
Even after R0 resection with perioperative FLOT, roughly half of patients relapse within 2–3 years. Distant metastasis (liver, lung, bone) dominates over locoregional failure in EAC — the inverse of squamous cell carcinoma.

Who gets CROSS?

cT2–T4a, N0–N3, M0. FLOT not tolerable (elderly, PS 2, pre-existing neuropathy, patient preference). RT-eligible anatomy. NCCN Cat 1.

Re-staging after CRT

CT ± EUS ~3–4 weeks post-RT. Goal: exclude M1. Cannot reliably predict pCR on imaging. PET metabolic response is exploratory, not standard for surgical decisions.

pCR (23%) — what it means

pCR patients have 5-yr OS ~74%, recurrence ~21% (most distant). pCR is NOT a validated OS surrogate and should not be used as a primary trial endpoint (Leite et al. Eur J Cancer 2026).

Adjuvant after CROSS

For EAC with ypN+ or R1: VESTIGE nivo+ipi (emerging European standard). No adjuvant chemotherapy is established. Clinical trial preferred.

ICI benefit is NOT uniform: MSI-H patients show dramatic pCR rates (~60–80%). PD-L1 CPS ≥10 patients show most consistent benefit. CPS <5: benefit marginal or absent. Do not use outside a clinical trial.

PD-L1 CPS selection

Most trials require CPS ≥1; benefit concentrated in CPS ≥5–10. dMMR/MSI-H tumors may be candidates for ICI-alone neoadjuvant (NEONIPIGA/INFINITY trials).

irAE management

Grade ≥3 irAE: hold ICI, start corticosteroids (1–2 mg/kg prednisolone). Grade 3 pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis: permanent ICI discontinuation per most protocols.

Active trials (2025)

DANTE (atezo+FLOT), MATTERHORN (durva+FLOT), KEYNOTE-585 (pembro+FLOT cohort), PHERFLOT (pembro+trast+FLOT for HER2+). Enrol patients where eligible.

MSI-H — different approach

MSI-H EAC (~3–5%): NEONIPIGA protocol — nivolumab + ipilimumab neoadjuvant (no chemo). pCR rate 58.6%. Consider ICI-alone approach in this rare subgroup.

Clinical challenge

How Resistance is Recognized

Resistance recognition in EAC is almost entirely retrospective and radiological. There is no validated on-treatment molecular resistance test in routine clinical use.

Neoadjuvant setting — the blind window
Resistance is inferred from the resection specimen after the patient has already completed 3–4 months of pre-operative treatment. Mandard TRG 4–5 (virtually no regression) or Becker Grade 3 are assigned on the surgical specimen. By the time this is known, the treatment course is complete. Approximately 40–50% of patients have TRG 3–5. There is no established switch protocol for non-responders before surgery.
Metastatic setting — RECIST as the standard
Resistance = radiological progression by RECIST 1.1 on CT, re-staged every 8–12 weeks. Symptomatic progression (worsening dysphagia, weight loss, pain) sometimes precedes scan confirmation but is not a formal switching criterion. Approximately 40–60% of patients treated with standard regimens do not respond — yet until imaging confirms progression, treatment continues. This means roughly half of advanced patients receive several cycles of ineffective therapy.
PET-adapted approaches — not yet mainstream
MUNICON/MUNICON-II trials tested early FDG-PET metabolic response (~2 weeks after first cycle) to identify non-responders and switch regimens. PERCIST was found to be a stronger predictor of outcomes than RECIST in esophageal cancer receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy. However, PET-adaptation has not entered mainstream practice due to logistical complexity and unclear survival benefit from switching.
ctDNA — promising but research stage
Early ctDNA dynamics (cycle 1–2) predict later chemotherapy response and OS in small cohorts (PMID 41256319, PMID 40275077). Low baseline cfDNA or high T-cell repertoire richness correlates with longer OS in 2L (RAP trial). However, no cut-off is validated, no commercial assay is standardized for this indication, and no trial has used ctDNA to drive treatment switching in EAC.
What clinicians currently do
At progression in the metastatic setting: re-biopsy is recommended but infrequently done in practice — HER2 status and PD-L1 CPS can evolve. Switch to 2L (ramucirumab + paclitaxel). Beyond 2L: irinotecan-based regimens, trifluridine/tipiracil, or clinical trial. NCCN recommends molecular re-profiling at progression if clinically feasible, but this is aspirational rather than routine.
The open gap
There is no validated on-treatment marker — molecular, radiological, or blood-based — that reliably identifies a patient whose tumor is becoming resistant during neoadjuvant FLOT or CROSS, in time to change treatment before surgery. This is one of the most significant unmet clinical needs in EAC. Circulating tumor cells, ctDNA kinetics, and radiomics-based models are all under active investigation but none has reached clinical validation.
